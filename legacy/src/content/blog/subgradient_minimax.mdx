## TL;DR

When solving minimax problems of the form $\min_x \max_y f(x, y)$, computing descent directions for the non-smooth envelope $F(x) \coloneq \sup_{y \in Y} f(x, y)$ appears formidable:
* Automatic differentiation cannot differentiate through an approximate argmax $y^*(x)$.
* Danskin's envelope theorem fails because $F$ is typically non-differentiable.
* Alternating gradient descent-ascent often diverges or enters limit cycles.

Here, we show a clean, constructive result: **the weighted average of subgradients produced while solving the inner maximization with dual averaging is provably an $\varepsilon$-subgradient of $F(x)$**, where $\varepsilon$ is exactly the duality gap of the inner solver. 

Rather than viewing the inner problem as finding an optimizer $y^*(x)$, the inner loop is an **$\varepsilon$-subgradient estimator**. As the inner solver runs longer, $\varepsilon \to 0$, producing a certified subgradient for the outer minimization.

---

## The Problem with Min-Max

Consider the minimax objective:

$$
\begin{equation*}
\min_{x \in X} F(x), \quad \text{where } F(x) \coloneq \sup_{y \in Y} f(x, y)
\end{equation*}
$$

with $f(\cdot, y)$ convex for every $y$, and $f(x, \cdot)$ concave for every $x$.

In practice, we cannot evaluate the exact supremum $y^*(x) \coloneq \arg\max_{y \in Y} f(x, y)$. If we solve the inner problem approximately, we cannot simply differentiate through the solver's iterations: the sensitivity of $y^*(x)$ with respect to $x$ does not vanish when optimality is only approximate.

The standard workaround—linearizing the game locally or solving an auxiliary direction-finding problem—is computationally expensive and provides no termination certificate.

---

## Constructive $\varepsilon$-Subgradients via Dual Averaging

Instead, let $x$ be fixed, and suppose an inner subgradient algorithm generates iterates $\{y_t\}_{t=1}^T$ with convex weights $\{\lambda_t\}_{t=1}^T$ ($\sum_{t=1}^T \lambda_t = 1$, $\lambda_t > 0$).

The algorithm achieves $\varepsilon_T$-accuracy in function values:

$$
\begin{equation*}
\varepsilon_T \coloneq F(x) - \sum_{t=1}^T \lambda_t f(x, y_t) \ge 0, \quad \lim_{T \to \infty} \varepsilon_T = 0
\end{equation*}
$$

Define the corresponding weighted average of subgradients with respect to $x$:

$$
\begin{equation*}
\bar{g}_T \coloneq \sum_{t=1}^T \lambda_t g_t, \quad \text{where } g_t \in \partial_x f(x, y_t)
\end{equation*}
$$

### Step 1: $\bar{g}_T$ is an $\varepsilon_T$-Subgradient of $F$

For any test point $z \in X$, convexity of $f(\cdot, y_t)$ guarantees:

$$
\begin{equation*}
f(z, y_t) \ge f(x, y_t) + \langle g_t, z - x \rangle
\end{equation*}
$$

Multiplying by $\lambda_t$ and summing over $t = 1, \dots, T$:

$$
\begin{equation*}
\sum_{t=1}^T \lambda_t f(z, y_t) \ge \sum_{t=1}^T \lambda_t f(x, y_t) + \langle \bar{g}_T, z - x \rangle
\end{equation*}
$$

By definition of the envelope, $F(z) \ge f(z, y_t)$ for every $t$, so the convex combination satisfies $F(z) \ge \sum_{t=1}^T \lambda_t f(z, y_t)$. Substituting $\sum_{t=1}^T \lambda_t f(x, y_t) = F(x) - \varepsilon_T$:

$$
\begin{equation*}
F(z) \ge F(x) + \langle \bar{g}_T, z - x \rangle - \varepsilon_T, \quad \forall z \in X
\end{equation*}
$$

By definition of the $\varepsilon$-subdifferential, this proves:

$$
\begin{equation*}
\bar{g}_T \in \partial_{\varepsilon_T} F(x)
\end{equation*}
$$

### Step 2: Squeezing into the Exact Subdifferential

Assuming bounded subgradients $\Vert{}g_t\Vert{} \le M$, the sequence $\{\bar{g}_T\}$ is bounded. Let $\bar{g}$ be any limit point along a subsequence $T_k \to \infty$. Taking limits:

$$
\begin{equation*}
F(z) \ge F(x) + \langle \bar{g}, z - x \rangle - \lim_{k \to \infty} \varepsilon_{T_k} = F(x) + \langle \bar{g}, z - x \rangle, \quad \forall z \in X
\end{equation*}
$$

Consequently:

$$
\begin{equation*}
\bar{g} \in \partial F(x), \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{T \to \infty} \mathrm{dist}\left(\bar{g}_T, \, \partial F(x)\right) = 0
\end{equation*}
$$

## Why This Matters

This simple result completely reframes the architecture of minimax optimization:

* **No Simultaneous Rotation:** Alternating descent-ascent methods often suffer from rotational dynamics and limit cycles. Decoupling the problem into an outer $\varepsilon$-subgradient descent loop and an inner dual-averaging estimator eliminates these rotational pathologies.
* **Principled Stopping Criteria:** The inner solver does not need arbitrary heuristic stopping rules. Because the subgradient estimation error $\varepsilon_T$ is observable from the dual bound, we run the inner estimator just long enough to satisfy the tolerance required by the outer step.
* **Acceleration and Momentum:** When $F(x)$ exhibits additional structure (such as strong convexity or smoothness), the inner estimation tolerance can be coupled to $\Vert{}\bar{g}_T\Vert{}$ to preserve fast or accelerated convergence rates in the outer iterates.
