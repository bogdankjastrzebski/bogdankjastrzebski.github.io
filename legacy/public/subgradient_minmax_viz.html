Inner subgradient MIN over $x$ · outer MIN over $y$ of $G(y)=\min_{x}f(x,y)$
inner iters = –
$ f(x,y)\coloneq -\lvert x-y\rvert+\tfrac12 x^{2} $ — solved on the box $ x,y\in[-\rho,\rho] $ with $ \rho\le1 $ (the panel takes $ \rho=1 $), so the $ \tfrac12x^{2} $ never overpowers $ \lvert x-y\rvert $: on that box each branch $ x\gtrless y $ has slope $ x\mp1 $ of a single sign, and near $ (0,0) $ the kink dominates — a genuine min–max up to an $ O(\rho) $ curvature defect ($ f $ is concave in $ y $ everywhere and convex in $ x $ only on the branches). The inner loop runs subgradient descent on $x$ (minimise; $ \partial_x f=-\operatorname{sign}(x-y)+x $ jumps by $ -2 $ across the kink line $ x=y $). Since the two branches bottom out at $ x=\pm1 $, the minimiser is $ x^*(y)=-\operatorname{sign}(y) $ and the value function is $ G(y)=\min_x f=-\tfrac12-\lvert y\rvert $: concave, with its \emph{only} kink at $ y=0 $, where $ \arg\min_x $ jumps from $ +1 $ to $ -1 $. The averaged $x_2$-direction subgradient $ \bar g_T $ (an EMA of $ \operatorname{sign}(x_k-y)\in\{-1,+1\} $) is certified against $ \partial G(y)=\{-\operatorname{sign}(y)\} $ — the interval $ [-1,1] $ at $ y=0 $ — via $ \hat\epsilon=\mathrm{dist}(\bar g_T,\partial G) $. The outer loop minimises $ G $ by descent, so $ y $ walks to the box edge $ \pm\rho $; the interesting point is the kink at the origin.

(a) the red curve is $f(\cdot|y)=-\lvert\cdot-y\rvert+\tfrac12(\cdot)^2$: on each side of the kink line $x{=}y$ (dashed) it is a parabola of curvature $1$, but the slope drops by $2$ crossing the kink, so $f(\cdot|y)$ is not convex on the box — it is minimised on whichever branch reaches $x=+1$ (when $y<0$) or $x=-1$ (when $y>0$). The green dot is $x^*(y)=-\operatorname{sign}(y)$, the blue dot where the inner descent ends up. At $y=0$ the two branches tie: $\arg\min_x=\{+1,-1\}$, $G(0)=-\tfrac12$, and $\partial G(0)=[-1,1]$.

(b) the blue line is the running averaged subgradient $\bar g_T$ — an EMA of $\pm1$ values, so it can only drift where the iterates cross the kink; the dashed orange line is the dual bound $E_k$. The estimator is valid exactly when the blue lies inside the green band $\partial G(y)=-\operatorname{sign}(y)$ (the segment $[-1,1]$ at $y=0$), i.e. when the fraction of iterates above the diagonal settles to $0$ or $1$. Away from the origin the EMA does lock onto $-\operatorname{sign}(y)$ and $\hat\epsilon=0$. At the kink $y=0$ the certificate is \emph{vacuous}: $\partial G(0)=[-1,1]$ is the whole segment, so even a $\bar g_T$ stuck at the wrong $\pm1$ (the inner descent can trap itself on the wrong branch of a nonconvex $f$) still reads $\hat\epsilon=0$. This is the misconception the example exposes: a certified $\varepsilon$-subgradient of a kinked value function can be useless, and an unweighted EMA has no reason to pick the useful element of $\partial G$.

(c) the inner maximization over $y$ at the selected $x_0$: the loop is normalized sign-ascent $y_{k+1}=y_k+a_k\operatorname{sign}(x_0-y_k)$ and the blue curve is the distance of the running average $\bar g_k=\beta\bar g_{k-1}+(1-\beta)\,\partial_x f(x_0,y_k)$ to $\partial F(x_0)$, where $F(x)=\max_{\lvert y\rvert\le1}f(x,y)=\tfrac12x^2$ on the box, so $\nabla F(x_0)=x_0$ (the segment $[0,1]$ at $x_0=\pm1$); orange is $\lvert y_k-y^*(x_0)\rVert$ and green $\lvert f(x_0,y_k)-F(x_0)\rvert$, all on a $\log_{10}$ scale. What makes $\bar g_k$ converge is that the ascent reaches the diagonal $y=x_0$: there $\operatorname{sign}(x_0-y)$ flips every step, the $\pm1$ contributions in $\partial_x f=-\operatorname{sign}(x_0-y)+x_0$ cancel and $\bar g_k\to x_0=\nabla F(x_0)$. The normalized steps travel a total $a_0\gamma/(1-\gamma)$, so with $\gamma=0.55$ you need $a_0\gtrsim0.57$ to reach $x_0=0.7$ from $y_0=0$: lower the ascent stalls on one side, $\bar g$ locks onto the biased value $x_0\mp1$, $\hat\epsilon$ sits at $1$ — and the $\text{stop}$ fires exactly as before, because it never looks at $\hat\epsilon$.
