Each step solves the tiny QP $\min\{\lVert g\rVert : g\in\partial f\}=$ conv$\{a_i:i\in A\}$, walks along $d=-g/\lVert g\rVert$ and takes the ratio-test step, i.e. as far as the active set does not change; then the face, the subdifferential and hence the direction are rebuilt. $\lVert g\rVert=0$ is the certificate $0\in\partial f$.

What the panel demonstrates. $f$ is a maximum of affine pieces, so its graph is a polyhedral surface and its epigraph a polyhedron. At an iterate the active set $A=\{i: a_i^{\mathsf T}x+b_i=f(x)\}$ pins the subdifferential $\partial f(x)=\operatorname{conv}\{a_i:i\in A\}$, and the steepest descent direction in the Euclidean metric is $d=-g/\lVert g\rVert$ with $g$ the minimum-norm element of that convex hull — a two-dimensional QP, the very object the restricted primal of the primal-dual scheme computes. Holding $A$ fixed makes $f$ linear along $d$, so the maximal admissible step is a ratio test: $t=\min_{i\notin A}\,(f(x)-(a_i^{\mathsf T}x+b_i))/(\max_{j\in A}a_j^{\mathsf T}d-a_i^{\mathsf T}d)$. At $t$ a new facet is tight, $A$ grows, the direction is rebuilt. Every facet that enters stays in, so the walk terminates in finitely many steps at the point where $0\in\operatorname{conv}\{a_i:i\in A\}$ — that single line is the optimality certificate, and the orange arrow is exactly how far $\operatorname{conv}\partial f$ sits from the origin.
